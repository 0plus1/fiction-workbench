# Scene and Prose Decisions

The author's approved pages outrank these defaults. Read sustained passages, not a few quotable lines. Borrow the freedom of their attention and syntax, not recurring metaphors or sentence templates.

## Showing, telling and emotional depth

Choose narrative distance deliberately. Scene gives a moment duration; summary crosses time; reflection lets the narrator understand or misunderstand it. A novel needs all three. Expand the exchange that changes a relationship. Compress the third repetition of an established routine unless this repetition changes it.

When an emotion feels asserted rather than experienced, find what it changes: a choice, what the person notices, the effort required to continue, an omission, a conflicting wish. A tightened jaw is not inherently more revealing than “I was angry”. Do not translate every abstract word into anatomy.

After an action or line, test the explanation that follows. If it only restates an inference already available, cut it. If it adds an association, contradiction, fact or self-deception specific to this narrator, it may belong. Preserve exact telling such as the approved Chapter 1 line, “The apology moved the entire conversation into the past tense.” A ban on reflection would damage this voice.

Show emotion over time as well as within a gesture. A person may act competently during a loss and fail at a small unrelated task later. Do not prescribe that response universally; find the behaviour this character would have. Avoid giving every character eloquent access to their own motives. An emotional scene need not end with catharsis, agreement or a lesson.

## Specificity and imagery

Give each scene a material activity or situation people can actually inhabit. Details should follow attention: expertise, desire, fatigue, embarrassment, habit or a practical need. A room inventory and a list of the five senses are not substitutes for perspective.

Before adding an image, ask what is actually being perceived and why this person notices it now. Let an exact comparison extend that perception. If it could be transferred unchanged into an unrelated scene, inspect it for decorative abstraction. Do not remove imaginative association merely because a literal sentence is available.

Precision has a cost. Which foot, which finger, the exact angle and every small movement are useful only when the action depends on them. Otherwise they slow the scene while pretending to clarify it. Names, objects and gestures should not accumulate only to supply recurring symbols.

## Paragraph movement

Compose a paragraph around what occupies the narrator, with room for that attention to change. Let syntax express relationships: an ongoing task can contain an interruption; a perception can prompt a memory; a thought can qualify itself before arriving anywhere. Give background action less emphasis than the thing that matters. Reflection is available, but a practical action or an absorbed observation can also sustain a paragraph without a lesson.

Before delivering a scene, read the opening and one sustained narrative passage against the approved voice. Ask:

- What carries forward across each full stop: a question, action, perception, expectation or thought? Where does the narration repeatedly finish one small item and start another?
- Which details receive more sentence space or emphasis than their importance warrants? Remove or compress expendable business before adjusting punctuation.
- Does a long sentence develop a relationship between its parts, or merely collect several small actions? Does a short sentence change the pace or just repeat the prevailing stop?

These questions diagnose an observed rhythm; they do not require every adjacent sentence to connect explicitly. Abrupt shifts, lists and short sentences can be deliberate. Check that the chosen effect suits the passage. Do not impose sentence-length averages, punctuation quotas or a compulsory alternation of long and short sentences. Read the revised passage in context, including the dialogue around it.

### Author-rejected example: the Anglesea opening

> We had bought enough chips for six people because the woman in the shop had asked whether we wanted a family portion and I had said yes. Now they covered most of the table. Malia opened the paper farther and put the fish in the middle while I searched the kitchen for salt. Every cupboard had a different way of opening. I found four wine glasses behind a door I had taken for the dishwasher.

The author rejected its repeated sentence/full-stop cadence. The sentences already differ in length and include subordinate clauses. The failure is the sequence of self-contained reports: purchase, quantity, serving, cupboards, discovery. Each item takes its turn at roughly equal narrative importance. The kitchen business expands without developing Marco's interest in it; the final discovery supplies a small comic finish without giving the paragraph a stronger reason to exist.

Revise the selection and relationship of the material. Decide what Marco is occupied with, retain details that develop that attention, and compress what merely gets dinner onto the table. A rewrite might stay with his response to the excessive food or follow the salt search as an interruption to something else. Neither is prescribed. Do not manufacture a memory, metaphor or philosophical conclusion to justify every domestic task. Simply joining these five sentences would preserve most of the problem.

This is a rejected sample, not a template to paraphrase. No replacement has yet been approved by the author. Preserve Chapter 1's effective short sentences and reflective freedom; this correction does not establish a general preference for long sentences.

Watch paragraph-level repetition too: repeated object/analogy/verdict structures or a neat closing observation after every mundane event. Let passages end where their attention has naturally moved, without manufacturing a punchline.

## Coherence without explanation

Know who is in the room, what can be seen or heard, who learned a fact, and what action made the next action possible. Select the necessary orientation; do not transcribe all choreography. An unexplained event can have clear physical staging. Magical realism does not excuse accidental geography, missing motivations or a narrator who knows another person's unspoken thoughts as fact.

Keep character change proportionate to its cause. Distinguish chosen ambiguity from a missing bridge. Preserve the narrator's limits rather than solving the supernatural with authorial explanation.

## Local calibration

In this project Chapter 1 is the approved narrative voice; Chapter 3's emails and property ads have their own deliberate register. Do not apply a ban on stock language to fictional estate-agent copy. Happy family and courtship scenes must have room to be happy. Current plot decisions govern the dog, children and supernatural timing; read those decisions rather than encoding a changing plot in this skill.
