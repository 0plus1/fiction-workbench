# Pattern Watchlist

Legacy filename; these are diagnostic patterns, not forbidden words. The author's approved prose outranks a watchlist match. Record why a passage needs revision before changing it. Explicit bans in a requested [constrained-drafting](constrained-drafting.md) profile are a separate, scoped requirement; do not waive them under this watchlist's exceptions.

## What the supplied benchmark list can tell us

The author supplied repetition statistics attributed to a creative-writing benchmark. Treat these as a prior about possible defaults, not a verified ranking, a complete corpus analysis or evidence that this manuscript has every weakness. Proper names may reflect recurring stories. Common spatial words and speech tags may be necessary. The excerpt alone does not establish frequencies relative to a matched baseline.

The actionable concern is repeated *behaviour in the prose*. Do not ban “beside”, “doorway” or “nodded” and then rotate synonyms. Avoiding every listed word can increase ornament and stiffness.

| Candidate pattern | Reader cost to inspect | Possible repair | Reason to keep |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Glanced, nodded, leaned, crouched, folded, grinned between almost every reply | Speech advances through stage directions instead of interaction | Remove filler; develop the conversation; keep an action that changes it | Necessary blocking, a particular recurring habit, an action doing real work |
| Right/left foot, forearm, wrist, two fingers, exact angles | Irrelevant anatomy masquerades as specificity | Describe the meaningful action at the right scale | Injury, technical task or spatial consequence needs precision |
| Tightening, trembling, breath, sternum as automatic emotion | Bodies become a standard emotion code | Find the choice, attention or consequence; or state the feeling plainly | A particular physical experience is the subject |
| “Could not decide whether”, “said nothing”, “neither moved” repeated | Held poses substitute for development | Decide what uncertainty makes someone do, or compress | A particular unresolved pause has pressure |
| Close enough to smell / stood close enough | Interchangeable intimacy | Let attraction follow this person's attention and the actual activity | The proximity or smell matters here |
| Not X but Y; not X, not Y, just Z | Repeated narrator correction creates predictable emphasis | State or dramatise the observation without the scaffold | An exact contrast or deliberate anaphora belongs to the voice |
| Three-beat lists and same-length fragment paragraphs | Chapter-wide rhythm becomes predictable | Revise the weakest cluster through selection and paragraph movement | An individual triad or short ending earns its emphasis |
| “The kind of…”, “as if…”, comparisons after every object | Every detail receives the same explanatory treatment | Keep the strongest association and allow literal observations too | A comparison develops the narrator's thought |
| “What mattered”, “the truth was”, “I understood then” at scene endings | Narration certifies a lesson already shown | End where the scene actually ends or keep only new thought | The realisation introduces a consequential new understanding |
| Vague “quality”, “something”, “presence”, emotional labels stacked together | The prose evades choosing what is perceived | Name the observation or meaningful uncertainty | Intentional unknowability or a precise abstract thought |
| Every room silent, every lamp flickering, every encounter portentous | Atmosphere replaces local facts and variation | Restore ordinary activity and selective attention | The actual scene warrants the condition |

## The explanation test

Weak: “I left the message unanswered. I was avoiding the conversation because I was afraid of conflict.”

Possible tighter version: “I left the message unanswered.”

A different valid version could retain a thought that changes the inference: “I left the message unanswered until I could make it sound as though I hadn't been waiting for it.”

These alternatives imply different motives. Choose from the actual character and scene; deletion is not automatically correct. Do not change story meaning to produce a prettier line.

## Review discipline

Look across a section, not just neighbouring sentences. Classify an observed instance as **keep**, **watch** or **revise**, with a reason. Repeated language may be a motif; repeated staging may be scaffolding. Judge function before frequency. Preserve ordinary vocabulary and the narrator's capacity for reflection. The ordinary watchlist imposes no word blacklist, numerical purity target or automatic replacement. Requested constrained drafting can enforce a separate literal blacklist without treating compliance as literary quality.
