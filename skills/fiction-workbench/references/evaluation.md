# Testing the Skill on Prose

Use for an author-requested experiment or when evaluating a substantial change to this skill. Do not automatically generate alternative chapters during ordinary edits. The author chooses what becomes manuscript prose.

## Make the comparison meaningful

Use a bounded scene of roughly 300–700 words, long enough for a conversation or perception to develop. Choose the same POV, facts, approximate length and source voice for alternatives. These are experiment parameters, not rules for prose generally.

Keep the question narrow. Compare, for example, a scene dominated by direct speech with one that compresses routine exchanges; or the same event with and without its explanatory ending. Do not confound a stylistic comparison by changing the characters' motives or inventing a new dramatic event in only one version.

For open scene development, alternatives can differ in incident or structure, but label that as a story comparison rather than evidence about a style rule. Preserve uncertainty about what the author will prefer. Do not nominate a winner by labelling one version “sloppy” before they read it.

Save temporary candidates outside the manuscript. In chat, use neutral A/B labels and include enough prose to judge, not just an account of which technique was used. Keep the comparison in the current agent unless the author explicitly authorises delegation. Structural validation of a skill is separate from a prose experiment.

## Reusable trials

Choose one relevant trial; do not run a whole suite by default.

**Ordinary warmth:** Two people making dinner discuss an incident from work. One is interested and tired at once. Nothing ominous must happen. Test whether each has a life beyond diagnosing the other and whether the conversation develops without polished banter at every turn.

**Practical disagreement:** Two parents discover that each expected the other to manage a pickup. Give the actual logistical constraint and preserve both people's reasons. Test whether the exchange changes through replies, without translating it into a lecture about commitment or padding every line with body movement.

**Uncertain perception:** A parent hears a familiar sound that could belong to a dead pet, then returns to an ordinary task. Do not confirm a ghost. Test whether attention changes without a portentous ending, repetitive grief physiology or an explanation of what the sound symbolises. This is an exercise, not adoption of the dog-bowl idea into book canon.

**Paragraph continuity:** Use the author-rejected Anglesea opening in the prose guidance. Offer a short replacement outside the manuscript that changes the selection and relation of details while preserving the basic dinner situation. Explain briefly what now carries attention across sentences. Do not count fewer full stops as success. Compare a second passage before claiming the correction transfers beyond this example; author approval is still pending until explicitly given.

**Reflective compression:** Several weeks pass between two scenes. Test whether summary preserves a narrator's personality without an inventory of milestones or a maxim after every detail.

## What to assess

- Can the reader follow what happened and why the next action follows?
- Does each speaker sound like someone pursuing this particular conversation?
- Can the reader infer emotion without the prose decoding every gesture? Does reflection add something when it appears?
- Which details or images could belong only here, and which could be pasted into any domestic scene?
- Does the paragraph rhythm resemble the approved narrator while allowing a different emotional register?
- Where did the author want to keep reading, and where did they feel the writing performing?

These are discussion questions, not an automatically scored benchmark. An AI-detector result may prompt review, but do not use detector optimisation as the writing objective or claim that a revision will pass one. Judge the passage against the author's stated objection and approved prose. Counts may expose a habit; they cannot choose the better scene. A single successful miniature does not validate a whole-chapter workflow.

## Learn from the result

Ask the author to mark actual lines they would keep and lines that feel false. Infer a narrow reusable preference from the contrast. Keep effective exceptions. Update guidance when the observation recurs; avoid adding a new universal ban after every dislike.

Report validation honestly: file structure checked, scanner exercised, prose trial performed, author preference received. Do not substitute one for another. A before/after test made by the same writer is a self-check, not independent evidence of improvement.
